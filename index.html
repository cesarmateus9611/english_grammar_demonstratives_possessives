Unit 1 · Grammar

Demonstratives
This · That · These · Those

We use demonstratives to point to specific things or people, showing whether they are near or far from the speaker.

📍 Near = THIS / THESE 🔭 Far = THAT / THOSE 1 thing = Singular 2+ things = Plural
The Core Rule
📏 Distance + Number

Demonstratives depend on two things: how far the object is from you, and whether it's singular (one) or plural (more than one).

THIS
Singular · Near
🖥️
"This is my laptop."
One object, right here.
1 thing · close
THESE
Plural · Near
📱📱
"These are my phones."
Multiple objects, right here.
2+ things · close
THAT
Singular · Far
🖥️
"That is his computer."
One object, over there.
1 thing · far
THOSE
Plural · Far
📱📱
"Those are her phones."
Multiple objects, over there.
2+ things · far

Real Examples

In a Tech Context

💻
This laptop has 16GB of RAM.
Este portátil tiene 16GB de RAM.
👉 One laptop, right in front of you.
📁
These folders contain the project files.
Estas carpetas contienen los archivos del proyecto.
👉 Multiple folders, nearby (on your screen).
🖥️
That monitor belongs to the teacher.
Ese monitor es del profesor.
👉 One monitor, across the room.
🔌
Those cables are tangled.
Esos cables están enredados.
👉 Multiple cables, far from you.
🐛
This bug is very hard to fix.
Este error es muy difícil de corregir.
👉 The bug you're looking at right now.
📱
Are those apps free?
¿Esas aplicaciones son gratis?
👉 Apps on someone else's phone, far away.
💡
BONUS — As Pronouns: Demonstratives can also replace a noun entirely:
"This is my code" · "Is that your keyboard?" · "These are the errors" · "Those are old files."
When used this way, they stand alone — no noun after them!
🤝 Special Use: Introductions

We use THIS and THESE to introduce people:
"This is my classmate, Andrés." · "These are my teammates."
Note: we do NOT usually use "that/those" to introduce people standing nearby.

Unit 2 · Grammar

Possessives
Adjectives · Nouns · Pronouns

We use possessives to show ownership or belonging. There are three types, each used in a different way.

Adj: my, your, his… Noun: John's, Maria's… Pronoun: mine, yours…
Type 1

Possessive Adjectives

Possessive adjectives come before a noun. They show who something belongs to. They do not change based on the noun — only on the owner.

📌 Structure

Possessive Adjective + Noun
"My laptop is fast." · "Her code has no bugs." · "Their project is finished."

🎯
Possessive Adjectives
Always followed by a noun
I →
MY
"My keyboard" Mi teclado
You →
YOUR
"Your screen" Tu pantalla
He →
HIS
"His code" Su código (él)
She →
HER
"Her project" Su proyecto (ella)
It →
ITS
"Its memory" Su memoria (cosa)
We →
OUR
"Our app" Nuestra app
You (all) →
YOUR
"Your files" Sus archivos (uds.)
They →
THEIR
"Their server" Su servidor (ellos)
👨‍💻
My program runs without errors.
Mi programa funciona sin errores.
👩‍💻
What is her favorite programming language?
¿Cuál es su lenguaje de programación favorito (de ella)?
🤝
We finished our app yesterday.
Terminamos nuestra app ayer.
🖥️
The computer lost its settings.
La computadora perdió su configuración.
Type 2

Possessive Nouns (the 's)

We add 's (apostrophe + s) to a person's name or noun to show ownership. For plural nouns ending in -s, we just add the apostrophe: s'

📌 Structure

Name/Noun + 's + thing owned
"Carlos's code is clean." · "The teacher's laptop is new." · "The students' projects are ready."

OwnerFormExampleNotes
Singular name Carlos's "Carlos's laptop is fast." Add 's after the name
Singular name ending in -s James's / James' "James's code is complex." Both forms are acceptable
Plural noun (ends in -s) students' "The students' projects are done." Add only ' after the s
Irregular plural children's "The children's apps are fun." Add 's (no -s in plural)
👨‍🏫
The teacher's computer is connected to the projector.
La computadora del profesor está conectada al proyector.
🏢
Google's new AI is very powerful.
La nueva IA de Google es muy poderosa.
👫
My teammates' code is better than mine.
El código de mis compañeros de equipo es mejor que el mío.
Type 3

Possessive Pronouns

Possessive pronouns replace the possessive adjective + noun together. They stand alone — no noun comes after them!

📌 Key Difference

Adjective: "This is my code." → Pronoun: "This code is mine."
The pronoun MINE replaces "my code" — no noun follows it.

🎯
Possessive Pronouns
Stand alone — no noun after them
I → my →
MINE
"That laptop is mine." Es mío/mía
You → your →
YOURS
"This cable is yours." Es tuyo/tuya
He → his →
HIS
"The mouse is his." Es suyo (él)
She → her →
HERS
"This desk is hers." Es suyo (ella)
It → its →
—
No pronoun form for "it" typically used
We → our →
OURS
"The project is ours." Es nuestro
You → your →
YOURS
"These files are yours." Son suyos (uds.)
They → their →
THEIRS
"The server is theirs." Es suyo (ellos)
💻
A: "Is this your laptop?" B: "Yes, it's mine."
"¿Es tu portátil?" "Sí, es mío."
📁
This folder is hers, not mine.
Esta carpeta es de ella, no mía.
🏆
The best app is ours!
¡La mejor app es la nuestra!

⚠️
Common Mistake — IT'S vs. ITS:
"It's fast" = it is fast (contraction) ✅
"Its battery is dead" = the battery of it (possessive) ✅
"Its'" does NOT exist! ❌
